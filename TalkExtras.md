# Introduction

Alright so here's the thing. The talk you're about to hear is not the talk I thought I'd be giving. 4 months separate my initial abstract submission and us staring at each other right now. When I wrote that abstract I was absolutely HYPED, maybe even the lower side of manic might be appropriate here. 

I'm going to go over 3 projects that have had the most impact on my life when it comes to AI, specifically Claude Code. In between those examples, I'm gonna talk about some new shit i've learned in those aforementioned 4 months. It feels like a millennia ago. My talks are about perspective. None of you can possibly have mine and it's impossible me to have yours, that's what conference talks are all about. 

3 stages. How I used it as a "professional", how i used it as a "hacker", how I used it as a "person". I got a little something different out of all of them. I'm gonna include some slides with examples of all the hate and vitriol against AI and maybe we can talk a little bit about why. 

First of all, a huge thank you to Medusa, Tweety Fish, and John Threat from cDc. I got a to a point where I was looking for ways out of giving this talk because it just didn't seem special anymore. Everything changed, then changed again 100x over. Who really gives a fuck what I think or feel about a thing that's already so "old." But cDc gave me a really great perspective. And another huge thank you to Julie at SecKC for her research assistance and my leadership create at DivisionHex for making this speaking engagement possible with our CFP bounty program. This shit literally takes a village. 

For a lot of us in this room, this is just life now. We're in it, we are on the cutting edge of every new discovery and fuck up. But it's gotta be COMPLETELY baffling to people on the outside. The confusion and the speed of change is something we as hackers might be losing site of cuz our bar for those things so is much higher than someone in an accounting office just trying to get chatgpt to summarize an email.
